Shift Planner for pick and inbound

Enter the expected orders, picking speed, inbound work and your staff. The planner builds a weekly shift plan that finishes the work on time with the fewest paid hours, and tells you how likely the plan is to hold on a normal week.

Everything runs in your browser. Staff data never leaves this computer.

1. Expected work

Orders to pick and inbound labour hours for each working day. Leave a day at 0 if there is no picking or inbound.

2. Hours and rules

Shifts are planned inside these windows in 30-minute steps.

3. Staff

Contract hours are the weekly minimum the planner tries to give each person (soft) and the maximum it never exceeds. Untick days someone cannot work.

Will the plan hold?

An independent test with 2,000 simulated days of random order counts, order sizes (1 to 170 items) and picking speeds.

How it works and what it assumes

Method
  • Workload per picking day = orders × items per order × seconds per item, divided by the productive share of paid time. The plan covers the workload that is not exceeded on your chosen share of days, estimated by simulation, so a normal busy day does not overrun.
  • The planner then solves a mixed-integer program (HiGHS solver, running in your browser). It picks each person’s shift for each day so that, at every half hour, enough capacity remains to finish all work released from that point on, the minimum floor staffing is met, and contract limits and availability are respected. It minimises paid hours, then gives a small push towards fair Saturday rotation.
Assumptions to confirm
  • Inbound workload and window are placeholders (6 labour hours on Tue, Thu and Sat, same window as picking). Replace them with your real figures and the real inbound time.
  • Contract rules in the staff table are examples (full-time 20 to 27.5 hours over up to 5 days, part-time 4 hours up to 3 days). Edit them to match real contracts.
  • Part-time staff work one 4-hour shift on each day they are rostered. Full-time shifts run 4 to 5.5 hours inside the window. Daytime work outside the window is not planned here.
  • The tool does not check labour-law or works-council rules. Shifts here end by 22:00 and are at most 5.5 hours, but if you extend them, check the break and rest-time rules that apply to you.
Planning aid, not a guarantee. Review every plan before publishing it to staff.
